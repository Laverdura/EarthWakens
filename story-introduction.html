Prelude

The Spiritual Realm is Existence itself—everything that exists, from a lifeless rock to a living being. It has no location or spatial dimensions.

Imagine roots gathering into a body, with bark for skin, moss for tissue, and vine-like fingers reaching through the soil. This is the Earth Spirit: a genderless being emerging from the Living World and becoming aware of its connection to Existence.

It experiences union with the Spiritual Realm while remaining alive. Yet its physical form cannot last forever. Its longing to preserve that union leads toward the Fissure.

Bring your own beliefs, questions, and imagination. Whether you understand existence through G‑d, nature, or another connection, Earth Wakens invites you to explore what it means to you.

Within this story, the Spiritual Realm is Existence itself—boundless, infinite, and without a physical form. Everything that exists already belongs to it. Spirit does not depend on life.

The Earth Spirit is our main character. Long before life appeared, it already existed as rock. It did not need to recognize or name its existence to belong to the Spiritual Realm. Over billions of years, Earth became a Living World. The story follows that world toward awareness of what it has always been part of.

I am Jewish, and some of the imagery in Earth Wakens draws from traditions, such as the tearing of a person’s garment when a family member becomes deceased. That image of tearing the cloth of a person’s garment carries into the story in a chapter titled Fissure. This is when the Earth Spirit tears itself into two separate entities of different genders, man and woman. The Earth Wakens card game is being designed to open up your imagination, inviting you to explore your own existence and discover what these ideas mean to you.

I want to concentrate on the Earth Spirit uniting and becoming one with the Spiritual Realm and giving you a way to imagine it through illustration.

Existence before life

Different religions and philosophies offer different ways of understanding G‑d, nature, and existence. One close parallel is Spinoza’s philosophy, which understands G‑d and Nature as one reality, with everything that exists belonging to that whole. Earth Wakens brings these questions into a mythology built around one idea: whatever exists has a spirit. Here, spirit means existence—the connection between all things, whether a lifeless rock or a living being. You may understand that existence as G‑d, as nature, or in another way that is meaningful to you.

We are of the earth, yet the spirit imagined here has no location or spatial dimensions. We can describe a body and trace its roots. Can we describe spirit without giving it boundaries it does not have?

Is consciousness spirit, or is it how a being recognizes its existence? Does the mind’s eye discover a connection that was always there, or shape how that connection is experienced? The Earth Spirit, the Living World, and the Spiritual Realm invite these questions without requiring one answer.

Life before male and female

Over this immense span of time, Earth became a living world. Simple organisms multiplied, and through reproduction, variation, and evolution, life grew increasingly diverse. Eventually, mammals emerged.

The earliest life in this story begins through asexual reproduction, before male and female have any role. Life first continues through division and replication, without the need for a mate. Some later forms of life can also reproduce without a male: through parthenogenesis, including mechanisms such as automixis, offspring can develop from unfertilized eggs.

Earth Wakens does not begin with an Adam-and-Eve pair. If gender is assigned at all to the original life-bearing role, the story places the female first, as the bearer and continuation of life. The emerging Earth Spirit itself has no assigned gender. Its division into woman and man belongs to a later chapter: the Fissure.

Imagine the Earth Spirit

Life grows through countless connections. In Utah, the many aspen trunks of Pando share a single root system. What appears separate aboveground belongs to one living organism beneath the soil. That hidden connection gives the imagination a place to begin.

The queen ant and its colony offer another image of belonging to a greater living whole. Within our story, the Earth Spirit is part of the Living World, sustained by its life and activity. These comparisons help us imagine a central awareness gathering through many connections.

Now imagine those vast roots drawing themselves together—as though the earth were deciding, “I’m going to become an autonomous being, different from all the other animals of the world.”

Roots and twigs become bony limbs, with bark for skin and moss as tissue. Hands reach through the soil with vine-like, skeletal fingertips. The Earth Spirit loosens itself from the ground, almost detaching from the earth so it can move and reach toward the Spiritual Realm.

The Living World rises, and Earth wakens to itself.

When you enter the Earth Spirit chapter, the illustration shows my conception of this emergence. Bare roots gradually become the moss-covered being imagined within them. Let your own fingers become those reaching roots. Imagine the soil around them and the living connections that allow this body to free itself from the confinement of the earth and walk upon it on two legs.

More about the imagined body and evolution

Within this mythology, the body develops directly from the Living World, independently of apes or any other animal lineage. In its earliest forms, any surviving traces would appear to be roots, branches, or other plant remains. This imagined origin belongs to the story.

Common ancestry, or common descent, describes the relationship between modern humans and the other living great apes. Humans do not descend from modern chimpanzees or gorillas. Instead, these lineages share extinct common ancestors—earlier primates that were neither modern humans nor modern chimpanzees or gorillas.

The precise identity and appearance of the last shared ancestor remain subjects of research. Common descent is supported by genetic comparisons, anatomy, and fossils. Earth Wakens uses that scientific account as a point of comparison for its own imagined origin: an Earth Spirit emerging through the living network itself.

Convergent evolution offers an analogy for this imagined emergence. Different evolutionary lineages can independently develop similar features, often in response to similar environmental challenges. Their resemblance comes from structures evolving independently, rather than those features being inherited from a shared ancestor. Earth Wakens extends that idea into fantasy: the living world yields a humanoid form able to reach, feel, and interact with its surroundings.

Earth and the Spiritual Realm

Its growing awareness reaches beyond what its hands can grasp. The Earth Spirit recognizes the Spiritual Realm as existence itself and experiences union with it while remaining alive. In essence, it becomes pure existence, yet it still has a physical form.

For a moment, imagine them as twins joined within one being: its earthly form and boundless spiritual existence, so completely united that the distinction between them dissolves.

The Earth Spirit has reached its nirvana. There is nothing more it could want, only the desire never to lose what it has found. It wants to remain this amazing living being while experiencing the infinite and eternity. Its longing for immortality grows from wanting to preserve both.

Yet the earthly part of itself, which it loves, remains something it does not fully understand. A root can wither. A limb can break. Its living form must eventually die. What becomes of their union then? This uncertainty gives rise to insecurity: the fear of losing the completeness it experiences within the Spiritual Realm.

In the Earth Wakens chapter, the changing illustrations carry that experience forward. Wooden doors fade into iron-reinforced doors as the Earth Spirit’s awareness grows. Its reaching hands express the longing to preserve a boundless experience through a living body that cannot last forever.

Toward the Fissure

In Jewish mourning, the tearing of a garment—or, in some communities, a ribbon—expresses grief for someone close. I draw on that image of tearing to give meaning to the Fissure in this story.

The possibility of losing its living union with the Spiritual Realm feels like a tearing within the Earth Spirit—a grief for a loss that has not yet happened. So powerful is its desire to preserve that union that it divides itself into two beings: woman and man. This is the Fissure.

Go to the Fissure

Through their union, new life can renew the living connection that one body cannot preserve indefinitely. Continuity through another life becomes its earthly answer to the longing for immortality. Humanity inherits that longing, its love of life, and its questions about what follows death.

Through love, memory, belief, and the lives that follow, each person reaches toward something that may endure. Whether that preserves the same awareness remains one of the story’s open questions. Our search continues through discovery and eventually artificial intelligence. Preserving our words may leave a trace of us. Whether it preserves the experience of being us remains a question.

Your own existence

Earth Wakens is also a card game of imagination and self-discovery. The Earth Spirit, the Living World, and the Spiritual Realm are parts of the world you enter through it. The invitation is to consider what gives your own existence meaning and explore your sense of spiritual self.

Bring your own beliefs, questions, and imagination. As you move through the story and its illustrations, picture yourself as that being of roots, bark, and moss—alive within the earth, reaching toward the infinite.
