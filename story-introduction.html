Earth Wakens · The Prelude

Prelude of the story

Before roots reached through soil, before anything swam through its waters or stretched toward its sunlight, Earth was a lifeless world. There were no eyes to see it, no hands to touch it, and no mind to wonder what it might become.

Yet it belonged to Existence.

In Earth Wakens, Existence is the Spiritual Realm.

It encompasses the living and the lifeless, everything we can perceive and everything we have yet to imagine. It does not wait for someone to believe in it. Earth belonged to it long before there was anyone to ask why.

This is where our story begins—with the question beneath the picture of a lifeless rock:

Can a rock have a spirit?

Within this story, even the lifeless Earth has spirit because it exists. But existence and the awareness of existence are different things. The rock does not know that it exists. It has no desire to become anything else.

Across an immense passage of time, Earth changes. Its surface cools. Water gathers. Life takes hold.

The rock becomes the Living World.

Life multiplies, takes new forms, and finds new ways to continue. Long before the story reaches the division into man and woman, the Living World is already creating and renewing itself. Its beginnings do not depend upon a human family or a human understanding of birth.

Here we encounter Mother Earth: the Earth that sustains life, receives what dies, and yields the substance from which life can grow again.

Nothing has to contemplate this cycle for it to continue. A root takes hold. A creature feeds. A body returns to the soil. What nourished one life becomes part of another.

Until the Earth Spirit wakens to its own existence, it belongs to this Living World in harmony with the Spiritual Realm. It does not stand apart from that belonging and consider what it means.

Then something begins to change.

The illustrations invite you to follow that change beneath the surface.

At first, you see roots, soil, bark, and tangled wood. Look again. A root begins to suggest a limb. Twigs gather into the shape of fingers. A mass that seemed fixed within the ground begins to suggest a body.

Limbs become bones. Twigs become hands.

And hands begin to reach.

These images give the Earth Spirit a form through which its story can be seen. The Living World is becoming capable of recognizing itself.

An image from the natural world helps us imagine that connection. In Utah, the aspen colony called Pando has many trunks joined through a shared root system. Above the ground, we see separate trees. Beneath it, their connection tells a larger story.

Now imagine looking at the Earth Spirit in that way. The figure emerging from the roots belongs to something much larger than the body we can see.

An ant colony offers another comparison. Our attention may settle on its queen, yet the colony’s life depends upon the activity of countless connected creatures. In this mythology, that relationship helps us picture an awareness gathering within the larger Living World.

The queen is a comparison, not a literal explanation of a single mind directing every ant. Likewise, the Earth Spirit does not control every creature, every growth, or every outcome. It belongs to the life from which its awareness emerges.

The story follows that awareness as it becomes more distinct.

What once responded begins to feel. What feels begins to recognize. Eventually, the Earth Spirit can perceive something that changes its relationship with everything around it:

I am.

I exist.

This is the wakening.

Earth has not been sleeping. The Living World has been growing and changing for ages. Now, through the Earth Spirit, it can contemplate its own existence.

The root mass gives that moment a visible body. The Earth Spirit can reach, touch, move, and encounter the world through a form it begins to recognize as its own.

The story leaves room for readers with different understandings of evolution. Its roots becoming limbs belong to its mythology. You may bring your own understanding of how bodies developed to the images. The question running through them remains: what happens when a being recognizes that it exists?

With that recognition comes a discovery.

The Earth Spirit perceives the Spiritual Realm with which it has always been joined. Existence has not suddenly appeared. The Earth Spirit has become aware of its belonging.

Yet the body through which it makes this discovery also confines it.

A root can wither. A limb can break. A living body can perish.

Before the wakening, life, death, and return belonged to the continuing rhythm of the Living World. Now the Earth Spirit knows itself as an individual presence within that rhythm. Returning to the earth means surrendering the form through which it experiences being itself.

That is why immortality matters.

The Earth Spirit wants both: to remain of Earth and the Living World, experiencing life through its body, and to become one with the Spiritual Realm—unconstrained, immortal, infinite, and eternal.

That desire gives meaning to the illustrations.

The skeletal hands and bony roots express a being bound within earthly substance. Its reaching expresses an awareness that can perceive more than its body allows it to experience.

The barred doors give that confinement another form. They represent a boundary the Earth Spirit can recognize but cannot yet overcome. The view beyond them offers an image of harmony and possibility.

No picture can fully portray Existence. A picture needs edges, shapes, and a point of view. The Spiritual Realm cannot be contained within those limits. The illustrations give us something through which to approach the idea.

When the Earth Spirit and the Spiritual Realm draw together, they are like twins—distinct, yet so completely joined that their separation becomes difficult to perceive. Within the mythology, even the angels cannot tell them apart.

One knows life through a changing body. The other is boundless Existence. Their union allows the Earth Spirit to experience a connection beyond the limits it has begun to fear.

But its earthly body remains vulnerable.

The story carries that longing toward the Fissure.

Until this division, the Earth Spirit is genderless. Through the Fissure, it becomes man and woman. What had been joined within one being now encounters itself through another.

Separation brings the desire for reunion. Union brings the possibility of new life.

The Earth Spirit’s search for immortality now takes an earthly path: life continuing beyond the span of a single body. Yet another question follows. If life continues through someone else, has the individual remained—or has something new begun?

Humanity inherits that question.

We inherit bodies that let us experience the Living World, love for the lives we know, and an awareness that those lives can end. We build, remember, discover, believe, and try to preserve something of ourselves.

Each chapter follows another part of that journey. The Spiritual Realm establishes Existence. The Living World shows Earth becoming filled with life. The Earth Spirit gives that life a presence capable of knowing itself. The wakening reveals both its belonging and its confinement. The Fissure carries its longing into the human experience.

Eventually, the search reaches our own time and artificial intelligence.

We can preserve a voice, an image, words, and patterns of thought. We can create something that appears to answer as we would. But does preserving those things preserve the experience of being us?

The Earth Spirit’s oldest desire follows us into our newest creations.

As you read, return to the illustrations. Notice where the figure remains entangled in the earth. Notice where a hand has formed, where a body begins to separate from its surroundings, and where its attention seems to reach beyond what it can touch.

The pictures tell part of the story before the words explain it.

And when the card game enters that story, existence becomes personal. The choices you gather ask what gives your own existence meaning—what you value, what connects you to others, and what you hope might endure.

For now, begin with the roots.

Something within the Living World has come to know that it exists.

And it has begun to reach.

Read the story before you play—that is the first rule of the game.
